Background & Significance

Current robot manipulation benchmarks primarily evaluate whether a policy can complete a task under a fixed set of initial conditions. This setting is useful for measuring basic competence, but it does not fully capture the demands of physical interaction. During interaction, hidden physical parameters such as friction, mass, and air resistance cannot be observed directly; their effects appear in the outcomes of the robot's actions.

ReAct-Manip is designed to measure the capability that follows: whether a robot can recognize that its current plan is no longer sufficient, use new observations to update its behavior, and recover without relying on a task-specific reset or privileged intervention. By treating adaptation as part of manipulation rather than as an exceptional failure case, the benchmark provides a more realistic measure of robustness for physical AI systems.

Task Specification

Each ReAct-Manip episode follows three stages: START with the scene and instruction, ADAPT to hidden physical parameters, and PERFORM the task.

01

START

The initial scene and instruction define the manipulation objective and the available observations.

02

ADAPT

Learn to adapt hidden parameters such as friction, mass, and air resistance through their effects on physical interactions.

03

PERFORM

Use the adapted behavior to carry out the task and work toward the manipulation objective.

04

Success criteria

We compare task completion, adaptation, and the additional time or intervention needed to reach the goal.

Details ↗
Task Specification

START provides the initial scene and instruction. During ADAPT, the policy uses observations of its interactions to learn how to handle unobservable physical parameters, including friction, mass, and air resistance. During PERFORM, the policy applies its adapted behavior to complete the task.

A complete task specification contains the initial scene and instruction, the robot embodiment and action space, the hidden physical parameters, the available observations, and the success criteria. The evaluation considers whether the robot can learn to adapt hidden parameters and perform the task with minimal additional time or intervention.
