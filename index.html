01

Background & Significance

Current robot manipulation benchmarks primarily evaluate whether a policy can complete a task under a fixed set of initial conditions. This setting is useful for measuring basic competence, but it does not fully capture the demands of physical interaction. In the real world, perception is incomplete, contact dynamics are uncertain, objects move, and task conditions can change after execution has already begun.

ReAct-Manip is designed to measure the capability that follows: whether a robot can recognize that its current plan is no longer sufficient, use new observations to update its behavior, and recover without relying on a task-specific reset or privileged intervention. By treating adaptation as part of manipulation rather than as an exceptional failure case, the benchmark provides a more realistic measure of robustness for physical AI systems.

02

Task Specification

Each ReAct-Manip episode begins with a manipulation objective, an initial observation, and a nominal execution condition. After the policy has started acting, the episode introduces a controlled change that was not specified in the initial instruction. The change may affect visual appearance, object pose, contact dynamics, target location, or the degree of task completion.

A complete task specification contains the initial scene and instruction, the robot embodiment and action space, the perturbation event and its timing, the available observations, and the success criteria. Policies are evaluated in both nominal and perturbed conditions. The central comparison is not only whether the robot succeeds, but whether it can detect the change, adapt its actions, and complete the task with minimal additional time or intervention.

03

Task Spec Catalog

ReAct-Manip organizes the current CL-Manip suite into 16 core task specifications. Robot-specific and interactive variants are treated as evaluation configurations of the same task family rather than counted as separate tasks.

01

Ball to Target Hole

Control a ball on an inclined board, hold it inside a target region, and guide it through a designated hole.

Balance · staged completion
02

Box Insertion

Push a block into a constrained slot under uncertain friction and asymmetric contact conditions.

Contact-rich pushing · alignment
03

Button–Indicator Matching

Use visual feedback to identify the button connected to a requested indicator and hold it long enough to score.

Visual grounding · sequential events
04

Conveyor Grasp

Sort moving objects by category into separate baskets while preserving the examples and avoiding mixed placements.

Moving objects · semantic sorting
05

Cup–Lid Matching

Match lids to cups by geometry and place each lid so that the final fit is physically stable.

Fine placement · object matching
06

Curling Push

Push a curling stone once, then release it and rely on its motion to settle inside a target circle.

Open-loop dynamics · release control
07

Find Object in Drawer

Open a drawer, identify the requested object among distractors, and place it into a storage box.

Search · articulation · retrieval
08

Laptop into Drawer

Grasp a laptop, reorient it through a restricted opening, place it inside, and close the drawer.

Articulated object · reorientation
09

Leveling Board

Select and arrange shims to level a board, then complete a physical verification step with a slider.

Multi-step assembly · geometric reasoning
10

Paddle Juggle

Maintain repeated ball–paddle interactions over time, recovering from changes in timing and contact state.

Continuous control · sustained success
11

Pan Flip

Use a pan to toss a rigid object, flip its face in the air, and catch it back inside the pan.

Dynamic manipulation · interception
12

Phone Power-On

Locate and press the power control on a phone under varying initial pose and visual conditions.

Precise contact · visual localization
13

Pitch Ball into Pot

Grasp a ball, swing through a release trajectory, and land the ball inside a pot.

Throwing · timing and dynamics
14

Rail Ball to Cup

Place a ball on descending rails so that it rolls through the valley, launches, and lands in a cup.

Indirect control · trajectory planning
15

Ring Toss

Release a ring toward a target peg and accumulate score across targets with different distances and values.

Goal-directed throwing · scoring
16

Rope Hang & Turntable

Handle articulated or underactuated objects: either swing a chain over a bar, or calibrate a rotating scoring surface and release at the right time.

Underactuated dynamics · online calibration
